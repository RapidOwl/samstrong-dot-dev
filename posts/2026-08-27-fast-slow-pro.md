This is the first in a series about things in our house that I love. You'll often see posts asking "what do I buy that will change my life?". I prefer "what have you bought that you loved and made a positive impact on your life?".

After cooking last night, I felt the need to rave about the Sage Fast Slow Pro. It's an electric, automatic pressure cooker (it can also slow cook and do other stuff, but the pressure cooking is the main thing) that takes all of the complexity out of pressure cooking. You put your food in, you choose a setting, you press go. It cooks your food.

So, there we were prepping an old, well-loved recipe. I was sweating the veg and browning the meat and adding the stock when I got to the part of the recipe that said "Now simmer for two hours". I think not. That would push dinner well past my daughter's bed time. I pull the Fast Slow Pro out of the cupboard and 45 minutes later, dinner is done.

So that's the Fast Slow Pro. Quite useful.

## Pros

- No stir risotto in 12 minutes.
- Dal in 20 minutes.
- Amazing chilli or bolognese in 30 minutes.
- Pulled pork in 1 hour.
- This is all fire and forget. You seal the pot, select the mode and press go.
- The lid and rubber seal are dishwasher safe so cleaning those is low effort.

## Cons

- The cooking times don't include time to get up to pressure. This can add 15 minutes sometimes, but it's still brilliant.
- This thing isn't really an all in one. If you want that glorious maillard reaction then you'll need to sear your ingredients in a pan that isn't a large chimney and transfer to the Fast Slow Pro for the pressure cooking phase. Thermodynamics only bends so far I guess.
- The knobs and buttons don't feel as premium as the cooking results. A top tier device like this should have an audible clunk when you turn a dial and clickety buttons. Instead, the knobs feel plasticky and wobbly and the plastic coating that covers the low profile buttons has started to bubble.
