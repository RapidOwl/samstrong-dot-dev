How much is enough? When do you hit diminishing returns? What brings you joy?

Take phone upgrades. You get a better camera, a new battery (that now matches how long your old phone battery lasted when you first got that phone, before the major OS updates wrecked it), probably a new colour (hidden inside a case) etc etc.

You restore your backup, go on abour your day, briefly marvel at how it's a little snappier. Then everything is the same. Are you satisfied? You have to convince yourself that you are because these things aren't cheap.

Anyway...

It is every consumer companies goal to raise the limits of satisfaction in some way. They rely on you thinking that your life would be improved if only you bought Product v4 and v5 and so on.

If you remain satisfied, you spend less money and this whole thing breaks down. It is in capital's interest to keep you dissatisfied in some way. So they can continue to grow. Endlessly.

And for the low, low price of {whatever} you can have literally the best phone in the world. There is no billionaire phone. There is no set of specs that are only affordable by the ultra-rich. It is in their interests to keep the best things vaguely affordable for the consumer class.

And endless growth isn't necessary. What do you actually need in order to be satisfied? Comfort? Safety? Health? Joy? What about that requires you to keep buying a new phone every year or three?

Now occasionally there are things you can buy that genuinely change your life, but those things generally don't come with yearly incremental upgrades. Then suddenly they do. Buy it for life slowly goes away.

Subscribe for life!

And our brains aren't good at coping with incremental change. We normalise things at an astonishing rate when those things are endlessly forced in front of us.

Comfort, safety, health, joy. These are the things they want us to struggle to maintain. Because they want to use money as a means of keeping score. Abominable.

A friend once said to me that he considered himself rich because he could go into a grocery store and buy whatever he wanted. He was correct.

The limits of satisfaction keep shifting. Capital shifts them on purpose.

Comfort, safety, health, joy.

Keeping running little hamster.
